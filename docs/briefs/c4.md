## Goal

SPEC Part II, "C4 · Mesh": services find each other by name, talk over authenticated, encrypted
links, and only along declared grants. Every workload gets an identity from the control plane,
bound to its service name, image digest and placement epoch. Grants such as `web → db:5432` are
compiled into per-node rules, and the default is deny. Local mode gets the single-node form.

The demo (SPEC, in substance; the Zigbox VMs are the machines):

1. Deploy `web` and `db` on different nodes, with the grant `web → db:5432`. `web`'s page shows
   data from `db`.
2. Exec into a third service, `batch`, and try `db:5432`. The connection is refused, and
   `zcube why` names the missing grant.
3. Add the grant `batch → db:5432` live: the same call now succeeds, and nothing restarts.
   Remove the grant: `batch` is cut off again, including a connection it already had open.
4. Capture traffic between the nodes with `tcpdump` on the host. It holds only TLS records, and a
   marker string stored in `db` never appears in it.
5. **(C3's deferred criterion, O4.)** A client calls `web` through the mesh at the demo load while
   `web` rolls out from `v1` to `v2` (C3's rolling strategy). The client counts zero failed
   requests.

Local mode (SPEC): `zcube local up`, then apply an app and a Postgres on one machine, with the app
granted `db:5432`. The app reads from the database. Without the grant, the connection is refused
and `zcube why` names the missing grant. There is no WireGuard and no second node.

Acceptance (SPEC, verbatim):

- "Grants change within seconds, cluster-wide, without restarts."
- "A workload's identity changes when its digest changes."
- "Every identity carries the placement epoch of the replica it was issued to; grants ignore it
  until C7 enforces it for stateful services."

## Non-goals

- **WireGuard** (O1). The SPEC's scope names WireGuard tunnels between nodes. This brief
  recommends mutual TLS per connection instead, and no WireGuard in C4. Whether the SPEC changes
  is the owner's call.
- **UDP, IPv6 and protocols other than TCP between workloads.** A grant names a TCP port. Zigbox
  M8b is IPv4 loopback only; so is this.
- **Enforcing the placement epoch.** It is carried in every identity and shown by `zcube why`.
  Grants ignore it until C7.
- **Edge and public traffic.** That is C5. C5's edge enters the mesh through a grant like any other
  caller, and C4 leaves room for that: an edge is one more grant source (see Interactions).
- **Egress to the internet or to hosts outside the cluster.** B3's relay covers jobs. A service's
  only route out is a granted service.
- **Layer-7 policy**: HTTP paths, methods, retries of requests and rate limits. A grant is
  (source service, destination service, port).
- **Grants scoped to signed versions.** The live doc says a digest-bound identity makes this
  possible. C4 carries the digest in the certificate. Grant syntax that names digests or
  signers is C6 (Trust) together with B5.
- **The Jepsen battery.** That is a C5 prerequisite, kept out of tree
  (`docs/decisions/2026-11-03-jepsen-before-c5.md`). C4 is gated by in-tree checks (see
  "Simulation and fault checks").
- **HTTP health checks.** A TCP check is in (O5). An HTTP check waits until something needs it.
- **`zcube exec`.** The demo execs into `batch` with `zbx exec` on the node. A cluster-wide exec
  command is not C4's.
- **Observed denials.** `zcube why` explains a refusal from policy (which grant is missing). C4
  does not record each refused connection attempt as a decision.
- **Load balancing beyond "a ready replica, local first, then by name hash".** No weights and no
  outlier detection.

## What exists, and what is missing (gap analysis)

### In Zigcube

- **Containers have no network.** `ZigboxRuntime` passes no `Network` option, so every container
  is `--net none`: it has loopback only. B3 gave a relayed job one way out: its supervisor holds a
  loopback listener (`ZCUBE_RELAY`, `127.0.0.1:8080`) made by Zigbox M8b before `execve`, and
  copies to one configured upstream (`src/relay.zig`, `relay_pump.zig`, `zigbox_supervisor.zig`).
  There is no way *into* a container, and nothing resolves a name.
- **The relay is the egress half of a mesh.** It has a listener inside the container and
  a pump in the supervisor (bounded by `relay_connections_max` 16, `relay_idle_ms`,
  `relay_buffer_bytes`). It reports failure as ZC-E0701–E0705 and W0701–W0702. Since B4 it
  also has a TLS client with a workload certificate. That holds on paper only: B4 is briefed,
  not built. The relay is per job, has one upstream, and is fixed at start.
- **Identity.**
  - Control-plane mTLS (node certificates, the cluster CA, `x509`, `tls_conn.zig` as a client,
    the identity extension at OID 2.25.192541954756406065736426278304158759968, which carries
    digest, epoch and labels) is merged. See `docs/mtls.md`.
  - The `workload` certificate kind accepts `spiffe://<cluster>/service/<name>`
    (`docs/decisions/2026-11-04-spiffe-service-path.md`).
  - B4 adds `/job/<name>`, issuance by the server for the current claim only (ZC-E0706), and a
    key that the supervisor makes and keeps in memory.
  - Nothing issues a *service* certificate yet, and nothing renews one.
- **No TLS server.** B7 builds one: TLS 1.3, Ed25519, x25519, AES-128-GCM, and client
  authentication always required. It is not built.
  - `docs/supervisor-handoff.md` says server-side TLS is open-sourced "by C4 if C4 picks mTLS".
- **No grants, no endpoints.** The service record has no grants. Nothing lists a service's ready
  replicas with an address. `/observed` has status (and C3's health, once C3 lands), but no
  address.
- **Readiness and rollouts are C3's.** C3 is briefed with the owner's answers and not started.
  The zero-errors demo step needs C3's readiness and rolling replace.
- **Simulators.** `sim-cluster`/`sim-c1` model nodes, a network, a store and jobs, with checks
  I1a–I7 and J1–J11, and B3's relay checks R1 and R3 under `--strong`. Nothing models
  connections between services.
- **Code range.** DIAGNOSTICS.md gives Zigcube `07xx` to the mesh.
  - B3 registered E0701–E0705, W0701 and W0702.
  - B4 plans E0706.

### In Zigbox (pinned v0.5.0, 4b3f6f9; read at `main` 82af2c7)

- **Networking modes** (M5, `docs/SPEC.md` Part I): `none` (the default, loopback only), `host`,
  and user-mode via pasta with `--publish`. Publishing implies user-mode networking, and so gives
  the container egress. That breaks default deny, so C4 does not use it.
- **M8b listener handoff** (`docs/briefs/M8b-listen.md`,
  `docs/decisions/2026-11-10-listener-handoff-m8b.md`):
  - The setup child makes one listening socket after it brings up loopback and before `execve`,
    and sends it to the caller with SCM_RIGHTS.
  - Library `listen: ?Listen`; CLI `--listen ADDR:PORT --listen-send FD`.
  - Only with `--net none`, IPv4 loopback only, `listen_count_max = 1`, refused on macOS.
  - Codes ZBX-E0606–E0609.
- **What M8b cannot do for a mesh:**
  - It makes one listener, at start. A grant added live (demo step 3) needs a new listener in a
    running container.
  - It cannot make a socket that *connects* to a server inside the container, which ingress
    needs.
  - Both need a socket made in the container's network namespace after start (ZB-1).
- **Name resolution.** I found no Zigbox option to supply `/etc/hosts` or `/etc/resolv.conf`
  content to a container (ZB-2). The rootfs is the image's tree.
- **`exec`** (M6b, `src/zigbox.zig`, `runtime/exec.zig`) joins the container's namespaces, so
  `zbx exec` into `batch` sees the same loopback as `batch`.

## Design

### Names and addresses

- Every service gets a **mesh address** when it is first created: `127.77.<hi>.<lo>`, from a
  counter in the store. It is stored in the service record and never changes or is reused while
  the service exists. All of `127.0.0.0/8` is loopback on Linux, so binding any address in it
  needs no configuration. That gives 65,534 services, far above the cluster bounds.
  - The address is the same in every container, so a name maps to one address everywhere.
  - Reuse after deletion waits until no replica of the old service remains on any node, and at
    least 24 hours, the decision-retention window.
- **A name resolves through `/etc/hosts`** (O3). Each container's supervisor owns a hosts file
  listing every service name with its mesh address. Zigbox mounts it read-only at `/etc/hosts`
  (ZB-2). The supervisor rewrites it in place when services are created or deleted. glibc and
  musl re-read `/etc/hosts` on each lookup.
  - Names resolve for *every* service, granted or not. That is how `batch` gets "connection
    refused", not "unknown host", in demo step 2.
  - The name is the bare service name (`db`), plus `db.<cluster>.zcube` for scripts that want
    a qualified name.
- **Ports are the grant's.** For a grant `S → D:P`, the source supervisor listens on
  `<D's mesh address>:P` inside each replica of S. There is no port remapping: `db:5432` in
  the source is `5432` in the destination.

### The data path

```
 node A                                        node B
 ┌ web container (--net none) ┐                ┌ db container (--net none) ┐
 │ app → db:5432               │                │ postgres on 127.0.0.1:5432 │
 │   = 127.77.0.3:5432 ──┐     │                │        ▲                  │
 └───────────────────────┼─────┘                └────────┼──────────────────┘
        listener (ZB-1)  │                     socket (ZB-1)  │
 ┌ web's supervisor ─────▼──────┐   TLS 1.3   ┌ db's supervisor ───┴──────────┐
 │ grant check, pick endpoint,  │ ══════════► │ mesh port on the node:        │
 │ TLS client, cert web/digest  │  mutual,    │ TLS server, cert db/digest,   │
 │ /epoch                       │  workload   │ grant check, connect inside,  │
 └──────────────────────────────┘  certs      │ pump                          │
                                               └───────────────────────────────┘
```

1. The source supervisor holds one listener per (granted destination, port) inside its
   container. It makes them with ZB-1 when the grant appears and closes them when it goes.
2. On accept, it picks an endpoint of D from its current endpoint list: a ready replica, on the
   same node first, then by a hash of the source replica's name, so that load spreads without
   shared state.
3. It opens TLS 1.3 to that endpoint's **mesh port**, an address on the destination node that
   the destination replica's supervisor listens on. SNI names the destination replica. The
   client presents the source replica's workload certificate.
   - If connecting fails before any byte is forwarded, it tries the next endpoint, at most
     `mesh_connect_attempts_max` (3) times, within `mesh_connect_timeout_ms` (5000). Then it
     closes the accepted socket.
   - Retrying is safe at that point: nothing has been sent.
4. The destination supervisor's TLS server presents the destination replica's certificate and
   requires a client certificate. It checks the following, all against its own current view:
   - the chain, to the cluster CA;
   - the kind and path, `/service/<S>`;
   - that a grant `S → D:P` exists;
   - that P is the port this connection is for (the ALPN-free first record carries no port, so
     the port is part of SNI: `<replica>.<port>.mesh`);
   - that the replica is not draining.
5. It makes a socket inside its container with ZB-1, connects to `127.0.0.1:P`, and pumps with
   `relay_pump.zig`. Plaintext exists only inside the two supervisors and the two containers.
6. **Revocation.** When a grant disappears from a supervisor's view, at either end, it closes
   the listeners and every open connection on that grant within `mesh_revoke_ms` (1000) of
   seeing the change. The agent's watch is the path. "Within seconds, cluster-wide" is the
   agents' watch latency plus `mesh_revoke_ms`. The demo measures it, and M2 bounds it.

The source checks its grant too, so a lagging destination cannot extend access. The destination
checks independently, so a lying source node cannot widen it either. Either end alone is enough to
refuse.

**Why the destination's supervisor, not a node-wide ingress.** Each key stays in the one
supervisor that made it. A supervisor crash affects one replica. The mesh port's lifetime is
the replica's, like B3's relay. A node-wide ingress would hold every local replica's key and
would be a new long-lived daemon. HANDOFF principle 1 only admits supervisors.

**Mesh ports.**
- The agent hands each local replica a port from a configured range: `--mesh-ports A-B`, default
  `30000-30999`.
- The replica reports its endpoint in `/observed` as `node address:port`.
- The node address is the one the agent already advertises for C2's chunk server.
- A replica whose port cannot be bound is a start failure with a code, not a silent fallback.

### Identity

- **Key and issuance**, B4's mechanism applied to services:
  - The supervisor makes the key.
  - The agent sends the public key to the server.
  - The server issues only for the node that holds the replica's current assignment, at its
    current epoch. The refusal is ZC-E0706, B4's code, with a cause that names the service.
- **Certificate.**
  - `workload` kind, path `/service/<name>`, the identity extension with the replica's digest
    and epoch, the service's labels (bounded as B4: 256 bytes, refused at admission with
    ZC-E0130).
  - Lifetime `workload_cert_ttl_ms` (1 hour). The supervisor renews at half-life with a new key.
    A certificate that cannot be renewed before it expires ends the replica's mesh, both
    listeners and the mesh port, but not the replica itself, with W0703. The replica keeps
    running, and C3's TCP check (if declared) marks it unhealthy. Renewal is new. B4 jobs never
    renew.
- **Verification.** Both ends check the chain, the kind, the path, `not_before` and `not_after`
  on their own clock (`Clock`, with C1's skew rules). They also check that the peer's identity
  extension has a digest and an epoch.
  - The client also checks that the server's path is `/service/<D>`. A connection that reaches
    the wrong service fails closed.

### Grants and their compilation

```zig
// db.zon (O7: declared on the destination, recommended)
.{
    .name = "db",
    .image = "...",
    .allow = .{ .{ .from = "web", .port = 5432 } },
}
```

- **Grant objects are stored with the destination service**, outside the spec that bumps
  `generation`. `zcube grant add web db:5432` and `zcube grant rm` change them live, and so does
  an apply that changes `.allow`. Every change writes a decision with who made it, so
  `zcube why` can show when a grant came and went.
- **The compiler** (core, `src/mesh_plan.zig`) maps (grants, services, ready replicas, node)
  to that node's rules:
  - for each local source replica, the listeners it needs and each one's endpoint list;
  - for each local destination replica, the (source service, port) pairs it accepts.
  - It is a pure function with table tests and a fuzzer. The agent runs it on every relevant
    watch event and sends the difference to the affected supervisors (O2).
- **`zcube why grant <src> <dst>:<port>`** (and `zcube why connection`, an alias) evaluates
  the policy:
  - "no grant batch → db:5432 (ZC-E0707); db allows: web → 5432 (added by … at …)";
  - or, when the grant exists, which replicas are endpoints and why others are not.
  - `zcube why service batch` lists its grants out and in.

### Measuring zero errors (C3's deferred criterion)

C3 proved availability as "ready replicas never below the minimum at every store revision" and
left errors seen by a client to C4 (`docs/decisions/2026-11-05-c3-owner-answers.md`, answer 2).
C4 measures it. O4 is the owner's confirmation of this reading.

- **The client.** A `loadgen` service with the grant `loadgen → web:8080`, at a fixed load
  (`demo_load_rps` 20). Each request uses a new connection (`Connection: close`). It logs one
  line per request: time, status, latency. A request **fails** if the connection is refused or
  reset, or the response is not 200 within 2 s.
- **During a rollout** of `web` (C3's rolling strategy, `max_surge 1`, `max_unavailable 0`,
  with a TCP check on 8080), the demo asserts zero failures across the whole rollout, and at
  least `20 × rollout seconds × 0.9` requests made, so the load really ran.
- **What makes that true:**
  - endpoints are ready replicas only;
  - a replica chosen for stopping drains first: it leaves the endpoint lists, waits until every
    supervisor that had it reports the update (or `mesh_drain_ms`, 3000, passes), then stops
    accepting, lets open connections finish within `grace_ms`, then stops;
  - the source retries connecting (never a forwarded request) on the next endpoint.
- **Not claimed:** zero errors for long-lived connections across a rollout. A keep-alive
  connection to a replica that stops is closed. The demo's client does not keep connections
  alive, and the demo document says so.
- **In simulation:** check M5 (below), with a simulated client at every step.

### Health checks: TCP (O5)

Once ZB-1 exists, a TCP check is cheap. The supervisor opens a socket inside the container and
connects to `127.0.0.1:<port>`. It reports through C3's probe seam (`probe_begin`/`probe_poll`)
as a second probe kind: `health = .{ .tcp = 8080 }`. Exit 0 means "connected within the
timeout". C3's readiness rules apply unchanged.

### Local mode

- `zcube local up` already runs the server on SQLite and one agent on the machine.
- On Linux with Zigbox linked, C4 uses the same path on one node: two supervisors, TLS over the
  node's loopback, mesh ports in the range. Proving the one path twice is cheaper than keeping
  two paths.
- With `FakeRuntime`, containers share the host's network, so there is no isolation and no
  "refused". Local mode's C4 demo therefore requires the Zigbox runtime.
- On macOS, `zcube local up` refuses grants with one line saying why (M8b and ZB-1 are
  Linux-only).

### Simulation and fault checks

`sim-cluster` gains services that connect (`--mesh`, on under `--c4` and `auto`), and **`sim-c4`**
runs these checks at every revision, natively and as WASM, with a tooth each.

| Check | Statement | Tooth (test-only fault) |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | No connection is forwarded unless a grant `S → D:P` existed at a revision the destination supervisor had seen, and no later revision it had seen removed it | `mesh_no_dst_check`: the destination skips its grant check |
| M2 | After a grant's removal at revision r, no new connection on it is accepted once each end has seen r, and every open one is closed within `mesh_revoke_ms` of that end seeing r | `mesh_revoke_late`: open connections are left open |
| M3 | Every issued certificate names the service, digest and epoch of a replica that was current and assigned to the requesting node at the issue revision | `mesh_issue_stale`: issuance skips the epoch check |
| M4 | No connection reaches a replica that was not ready, or was draining, when it was accepted | `mesh_route_unready`: endpoints include unready replicas |
| M5 | During a rollout with no node fault, a simulated client at constant load sees no failed request | `mesh_no_drain`: replicas stop without draining |
| M6 | A certificate's digest equals its replica's digest; a digest change gives a new key | `mesh_reuse_key`: the new replica keeps the old key |

- The fleet gets `--c4` like `--c1`, plus a `mesh` fault profile (partitions between supervisors,
  slow agents, grant churn), and canaries for M1–M6.
- Plaintext on the wire cannot be simulated. It is checked by `mesh-smoke` (unit level: the
  supervisor's sockets only ever write TLS records; a test sends a marker and greps a capture
  of the loopback) and by demo step 4.
- **The Jepsen-style check is `mesh-smoke`** (`src/mesh_smoke.zig`, like `swarm-smoke`):
  - real `zcube-server`, three `zcube-agent`s with `FakeRuntime`, and `fault_proxy.zig` between
    supervisors (latency, cuts at varied byte counts, stalls, the black hole);
  - a client and a server workload recording every request;
  - a checker that every completed request was granted at the time and none crossed a revoked
    grant after `mesh_revoke_ms`.
  - It is not in `check`, about 30 s. The full Jepsen battery remains C5's prerequisite.
- **`test-store-faults` is unchanged.** The mesh adds keys but no new store behaviour.

### Diagnostics

Numbers are fixed in slice 1; the registry decides collisions. Proposed:

- **E0707**: no grant (the connection's refusal, and `why grant`'s answer). Blame: config.
- **E0708**: a peer's certificate refused: chain, kind, path, time or extension. Blame:
  environment, or internal when both ends are ours and agree on time.
- **E0709**: no ready endpoint for a granted destination. Blame: the destination service.
- **E0710**: a mesh port could not be bound. Blame: environment (the range is in use).
- **E0711**: ZB-1 failed: no socket could be made in the container. Blame: runtime.
- **W0703**: the certificate could not be renewed; the replica's mesh is down until it is.
- **W0704**: a grant names a service that does not exist (allowed, so order of applies does not
  matter; warned).
- **E0135**: a bad `.allow` entry (port 0, a port above 65535, a duplicate, `from` naming
  itself). Admission. Blame: config.

## Slices

In dependency order, as C3's brief: **[overnight]** can run unattended on the fake and sim
runtimes with no owner decision. Size: S under 300 changed lines, M under 800, L more. Each slice
ends with `zig build check` PASS on the reported commit. A slice that adds a key also passes the
store conformance suite on Postgres (`ZCUBE_TEST_POSTGRES` set).

**Prerequisites, not C4 slices:**
- C3 through its slice 8 (readiness, rolling, rollback) for slices 5 and 12.
- C3 slice 9 (the probe seam) for slice 8.
- B4 (job certificates, issuance, the supervisor's key) for slice 3.
- B7 slice 1 (the TLS 1.3 server state machine) for slice 6.

O6 says how to order them.

1. **Keys, records and docs first [overnight], M.**
   - Service record v5 (or C3's v4 plus `mesh_address` and `allow`), the mesh-address counter,
     the grant decisions, `/observed` gaining `endpoint`.
   - The writer table; the codes (registered, explained, goldens); every doc: keys.md,
     authority.md, api.md, cli.md, mtls.md's threat list.
   - *Tests:* old records still read; the address is never reused while a replica remains;
     admission of bad `.allow` (E0135).
   - *Mechanical:* fixture call sites, mock records, goldens, the `docs/keys.md` tables.
   - Needs nothing (C3 slice 5 if it has landed, to stack on its versions).
2. **`mesh_plan`, pure [overnight], M.**
   - The grant compiler and endpoint choice in the core list, with table tests for each rule
     above and a fuzzer over (grants, replicas, readiness, draining).

## How demo-c4 runs

- **Machines.** The three QEMU guests of demo-c2 and demo-c3 (`zigbox_vm.Session`), joined by a
  host bridge, with `zcube-server` on the host with SQLite and each guest running
  `zcube-agent --runtime zigbox`.
  - The SPEC's netem risk: the bridge gets a netem profile (`delay 20ms 5ms loss 0.5%`) for steps
    1, 3 and 5. The demo records it.
  - **Real Zigbox, not `FakeRuntime`.** Isolation is the point, and only Zigbox gives it.
- **Images**, digest-pinned and recorded in slice 12:
  - `db` is a real Postgres (an Alpine Postgres 16 tag) with one table holding a marker string;
  - `web` is a minimal HTTP server whose page runs one query through `db:5432`;
  - `loadgen` and `batch` are small shell images with a client.
- **Witness.**
  - The driver follows the store's watch from before step 1. It also reads each agent's mesh
    rules from `GET /v1/nodes/<n>/mesh` (debug, read-only) to time "grant seen".
- **Asserted:**
  - Step 1: the page's text holds the marker.
  - Step 2: `zbx exec` into `batch` connects to `db:5432` and gets "connection refused"; `zcube
    why grant batch db:5432` prints ZC-E0707 and the existing grant `web → 5432`.
  - Step 3: after `zcube grant add`, the same call succeeds within 5 s and no replica's
    supervisor pid or container changed. A connection held open across `grant rm` is closed
    within 5 s, and a new one is refused.
  - Step 4: a 10-second `tcpdump` on the bridge during step 1's load. Every TCP payload between
    guests on a mesh port parses as TLS 1.3 records (`tshark` or a small parser in the driver),
    and the marker never appears in the capture.
  - Step 5: zero failed requests and the minimum count, as above.
  - The certificates: `zcube why replica` shows digest and epoch, and after step 5 every `web`
    replica's certificate names `v2`'s digest.
  - The local stage: `zcube local up` on the host, the app and Postgres, refused without the
    grant, and served with it.
- **Not asserted (cuts, recorded in `docs/demos/c4.md`):** node loss under load (that is the
  simulation's and `mesh-smoke`'s); keep-alive connections across a rollout; UDP; IPv6.
- About 10 to 12 minutes, KVM job, 45-minute timeout.

## Zigbox requests

| # | Request | Needed by |
|---|---|---|
| ZB-1 | A library call that returns a new, unconnected socket (IPv4 TCP) made in a running container's network namespace, e.g. `container_socket(handle, .tcp4) -> fd`, usable many times while the container runs, from a multi-threaded caller (the supervisor), with codes in ZBX-E06xx. How Zigbox makes it (a single-threaded helper that joined the user and network namespaces, kept outside the container's pid namespace so the workload cannot signal or trace it) is Zigbox's choice. With it, Zigcube needs no change to M8b's `listen_count_max`. | slices 7, 8 |
| ZB-2 | An option to mount a caller-owned file read-only at `/etc/hosts` in the container (a bind mount, so the caller's in-place rewrites are seen), refused unless `--net none` | slice 7 |
| ZB-3 | Confirm that `exec` (M6b) joins the network namespace, so `zbx exec` sees the container's loopback | slice 12 |
| ZB-4 | Confirm that a socket from ZB-1 survives a supervisor's adoption by a restarted agent (it is the supervisor's, so it should; listed so the test exists) | slice 7 |

## Interactions

- **B3's relay.** It is the mesh's egress half for one fixed upstream outside the cluster. C4
  generalizes its listener and pump; it does not replace it. A relayed job keeps `ZCUBE_RELAY` on
  `127.0.0.1:8080`, while mesh addresses live in `127.77.0.0/16`, so they cannot collide.
  - The relay's bounds (`relay_connections_max` 16, the byte and idle bounds) stay the relay's.
  - The mesh gets its own bounds, `mesh_connections_max` (64 per replica) and the same
    `relay_buffer_bytes`. They count separately, so a busy mesh cannot starve a relay.
- **Zigbox `--net none`.** C4 keeps it for every container. Nothing in C4 uses pasta, `--net host`
  or `--publish`. A container's only connections to anything are its supervisor's sockets.
- **M8b.** C4 does not need M8b once ZB-1 exists: ZB-1 makes listeners too, and makes them at any
  time. B3 keeps M8b for the relay, unchanged. Moving the relay to ZB-1 is a later cleanup, not
  C4's.
- **B4.** C4's service certificates are B4's issuance with a second path kind and renewal. If B4
  slips, slice 3 waits. Building B4's issuance twice is the worse option.
- **B7.** The mesh's TLS server is B7's. Both ends use client authentication always. C4 adds no
  TLS code beyond configuration. The open-sourcing commitment in `docs/supervisor-handoff.md`
  ("by C4 if C4 picks mTLS") falls due with C4's release.
- **B5 and C6.** The digest in the certificate is what C6 will scope grants by. C4 does not read
  signatures.
- **B6 (placement by tenant).** A grant across tenants is a question for B6's brief. C4 grants
  are by service name only.
- **C5.** The edge proxy will be a grant source (`edge → web:8080`), a supervisor-like process
  holding a certificate of a new path kind (`/edge/<name>`, its own decision). C4's compiler
  takes sources as identities, not only services, so C5 adds a kind, not a mechanism.
- **C7.** The epoch is already in every certificate. C7 adds a check at the destination that the
  epoch is the replica's current one for stateful services.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Drop WireGuard from C4?** The SPEC's scope says "WireGuard tunnels between nodes" and leaves
   identity "mTLS per connection, or WireGuard-level" open.
   *Recommended: mutual TLS per connection between supervisors, and no WireGuard in C4.*
   - Encryption and identity come from one mechanism the project already has: the cluster CA,
     `x509`, the TLS client, and B7's server.
   - WireGuard would need root or `CAP_NET_ADMIN` on every node (against the rootless default),
     or a userspace WireGuard, which is a new crypto implementation to review.
   - It would encrypt between nodes but say nothing about *which workload* is calling, so the
     grant check would need an identity layer on top anyway.
   - With only TCP grants, mTLS covers demo step 4.
   - The cost: one TLS handshake per connection (Ed25519 and x25519, cheap), and no encryption
     for traffic that is not a grant (there is none: default deny).
   - If you agree, the SPEC's C4 scope and open question should be updated in the live doc. That
     is yours to edit, not mine.
2. **A mesh seam on `Runtime` (Ask-first).** May I add `Runtime.Spec.mesh` (the replica's mesh
   port and initial rules) and `Runtime.mesh_update(handle, rules)`, non-blocking, so the agent
   can change a running container's grants and endpoints? Nothing else about `Runtime` changes.
   *Recommended: yes.* Grants must change without restarts, and the supervisor is the only process
   that can act inside the container. The alternative is a side channel to the supervisor that
   bypasses `Runtime`, which the fake and sim runtimes would not see.
3. **How are names resolved?**
   *Recommended: a per-container `/etc/hosts`, owned and rewritten by the supervisor (ZB-2),
   mapping every service name to a stable `127.77.x.y` address.*
   - The alternative is a small DNS responder in each supervisor on `127.0.0.53:53`, with a
     supplied `resolv.conf`. That is closer to the SPEC's words "cluster DNS", but it adds UDP, a
     DNS parser facing the workload, and a second Zigbox request.
   - Programs that ignore `/etc/hosts` (rare: some static Go binaries with a pure-Go resolver
     read it too) are the risk.
   - If you prefer DNS, it is one extra M slice after 7.
4. **What counts as "zero errors under real traffic" (C3's answer 2)?**
   *Recommended:* demo step 5 as written. A `loadgen` service at 20 requests/s, one connection
   per request, through the mesh, across a rolling update of `web`. A failure is a refused or
   reset connection or a non-200 within 2 s. Zero failures and at least 90% of the expected
   count, plus M5 in simulation at every step.
   - Long-lived connections across a rollout are not claimed, and the README says so.
5. **TCP health checks in C4?** C3 deferred network checks to C4 (C3's answer 6).
   *Recommended: TCP yes (slice 8, small once ZB-1 exists, and the zero-errors step uses it);
   HTTP no.*
6. **Order and dependencies.** C4 needs C3 (readiness and rollouts), B4 (issuance) and B7 slice 1
   (the TLS server).
   *Recommended:* finish C3 first, as planned. Run B4 and B7 slice 1 before C4's slices 3 and 6.
   Start C4's slices 1, 2 and 4 in parallel with C3's later slices, since they touch different
   files except the record versions. Slice 1 stacks on C3 slice 5.
   - Release C4 on its own, not combined with C3: each has its own demo and fleet soak, and
     minimizing time to release (2026-11-11) favours shipping C3 when it is ready.
7. **Where is a grant declared?**
   *Recommended: on the destination* (`db.zon: .allow = .{ .{ .from = "web", .port = 5432 } }`,
   and `zcube grant add web db:5432` writes there). Whoever owns the data decides who reaches it.
   That matters once tenants exist (B6).
   - The SPEC's wording, "the app granted db:5432", reads as declared on the source. The demo
     works either way.
   - Declaring on the source would let any service author grant itself access.
8. **Certificate lifetime for services.**
   *Recommended:* `workload_cert_ttl_ms` 1 hour, renewed at half-life with a fresh key. Expiry
   without renewal takes the replica off the mesh (W0703) but does not stop it.
   - Shorter means more load on the server's signer. Longer means a stolen key lives longer. The
     key never leaves the supervisor's memory either way.
9. **Bounds** (new user-visible limits; the supervisor decides under delegation if reviewers
   agree, listed here because some are defaults users see):
   - `mesh_revoke_ms` 1000;
   - `mesh_drain_ms` 3000;
   - `mesh_connect_attempts_max` 3;
   - `mesh_connect_timeout_ms` 5000;
   - `mesh_connections_max` 64 per replica;
   - `--mesh-ports` default `30000-30999` (1,000 replicas per node, above `replicas_per_node_max`;
     slice 1 checks that constant and states it);
   - the address block `127.77.0.0/16`.
10. **Codes.** E0707–E0711, W0703, W0704 and E0135 as listed under Diagnostics, numbers fixed in
    slice 1.
    *Recommended: approve the set*; the supervisor fixes collisions with C3's and B4's
    reservations.
11. **Local mode needs Zigbox for the mesh.** With `FakeRuntime`, processes share the host
    network, so nothing can be refused.
    *Recommended:* local mode's C4 form runs only with the Zigbox runtime on Linux, and refuses
    grants with one line on `FakeRuntime` and on macOS. The SPEC's local demo is part of
    `demo-c4`.
