# C3 demo: what runs, what is asserted, and what is not

**`zig build demo-c3` does not exist yet.** The brief ("How demo-c3 runs") runs it on three QEMU
guests with real Zigbox (`zcube-agent --runtime zigbox`), `zcube-server` on the host with SQLite,
in a KVM CI job built on demo-c2's harness. That is the slice's deliverable and is handed to the
next session (see "What is next"). Until it runs green in CI, nothing marks C3 demonstrated.

What exists is **`zig build c3-smoke -Dbackend=sqlite|postgres [-Dtranscript=FILE]`**
(`src/c3_smoke.zig`, `src/c3_smoke_steps.zig`, `src/c3_witness.zig`): the same script on real
processes with `FakeRuntime`: `zcube-server` (`--lease-ttl-ms 8000`), three `zcube-agent`s and the
`zcube` command. It asserts every outcome, prints a transcript and kills everything it started,
even on failure. About 1.5 to 2 minutes. CI runs it on SQLite in the `demos (sqlite)` job when
`.github/ci-changes.sh` picks `c3_smoke`. It proves the control plane end to end (server, store,
agents, CLI, controllers) and nothing about Zigbox: a health check's verdict is scripted through
the agent's fault directory, not probed. It is a smoke, not the demo.

  `.github/ci-changes.sh` with its rule and table-test rows.

## The images (c3-smoke)

The demo writes its own `images.zon` into its scratch directory: three digests standing for
`v1` (`b3:51aa9c03`), `v2` (`b3:9d20c4f1`) and `v3` (`b3:7e3a0b52`). The app is
`sh -c "while true; do sleep 1; done"` with the check `test -f /tmp/ready` (interval 1 s,
failure threshold 2). FakeRuntime does not run the check: every replica is healthy unless the
demo writes `unhealthy` into `<state-dir>/faults/<replica id>`, which the witness does for every
replica created with the `v3` image.

## The witness (`src/c3_witness.zig`)

From before step 1 the driver follows `GET /v1/watch` and judges every revision of the service's
keys against the state before it, not by sampling:

- **R1** a revision that retires a ready replica of an older spec during a rollout leaves at least
  `replicas - max_unavailable` ready;
- **R2** live replicas never exceed `replicas + max_surge` while a rollout is active;
- **R3** a replica created runs its generation's image (the service's, or `/history`'s);
- **R4** a revision under a paused rollout starts and retires nothing;
- **R5** when a rollback completes, every replica runs the restored generation's image.

"Ready" is what the rollout controller counts: reported running and healthy. In step 2 the
witness also holds the floor "never fewer than 3 ready" at every revision. A `v3` replica reported
healthy fails the run. The last line reports the revisions judged, e.g. `R1 R2 R3 R4 R5 held at
each of 180 revisions; 17 replicas created, 12 retired, at most 4 live; 2 health faults scripted`.

## The steps of c3-smoke and what each asserts

0. **Start.** `zcube version`; the server, a fault proxy and three agents; the witness starts.
1. **v1.** Apply with a health check and `rolling, surge 1, unavailable 0, min ready 2 s`. Three
   replicas ready on `v1`; `zcube rollout status` says `app: generation 1, 3 replicas`. The demo
   then waits `min_ready_ms` (see the trap below).
1b. **Refusals**, by code and summary: `zcube rollback` with one generation (ZC-E0127), `zcube
   rollout pause` with no rollout (ZC-E0129), a check with `interval_ms = 0` (ZC-E0125), surge and
   unavailable both 0 (ZC-E0126). The service is still at generation 1 afterwards.
2. **v2.** The rollout completes on `v2`; the witness saw at least 3 ready at every revision and
   at most 4 live; status says `1 → 2`.
3. **v3 never becomes healthy.** The first `v3` replica reports unhealthy; `why service`, `why
   replica` and `rollout status` are shown mid-rollout. The rollout fails at `ready_timeout_ms`
   and rolls back on its own: generation 4 is `auto_rollback` restoring 2, exactly one `v3`
   replica was ever created, every replica runs exactly `b3:9d20c4f1`, and status says
   `2 → 4 (rollback): complete`.
4. **History.** `zcube history` shows `4*`, `demo@` and the reasons apply, apply, apply,
   auto_rollback; `rollback --to 99` is refused with ZC-E0128.
4b. **Rollback, paused; the agent restarted.** `rollback --to 1` says `generation 4 → 5, restoring
   1`. At its first healthy replica it is paused (`rolling_back → paused`, status says `paused by
   user`). node2's agent is killed with `kill -9` and restarted: the same containers, supervisors
   and command pids, and nothing started or retired while paused (R4).
4c. **Resume; the server restarted.** `resume` says `paused → rolling_back`, with fewer than 3
   replicas at generation 5. The server is stopped (SIGTERM, exit 0) and started on the same
   store; the same rollout (same `started_at_ms`) completes on `v1`.
4d. **An apply during a rollback wins.** `zcube rollback` (`generation 5 → 6, restoring 4`); at
   its first new replica, an apply of `v1`. Generation 7 is the apply, settles on `v1`, no replica
   of generation 6 is left, and `history` shows `7*`.

Then the witness's verdict and the cleanup check (no process the demo started is left).

## Traps and fixes found while building c3-smoke

- **A first rollout needs ready old replicas.** A rollout's `from` is the newest earlier
  generation whose replicas are ready (have run healthy for `min_ready_ms`). Applying `v2` less
  than `min_ready_ms` after `v1` settles gives `rollout 0 → 2` and a failure that rolls back to
  nothing. That is deliberate (Review B, SHOULD 4; the test "a first rollout over a generation
  that never became ready rolls back to nothing"), so step 1 waits `min_ready_ms + 500 ms`.
- **Health did not reach the rollout** (`f7157f3`). The rollout controller counted a replica
  ready from its status alone, so `v3` completed its rollout and retired `v2`. Now a replica of a
  service with a check is ready only while its report says healthy.
- **An agent restart's `unknown` freed old replicas to retire** (`a765bca`): the rule is the
  supervisor's, `docs/decisions/2026-11-15-c3-rollout-health-gate.md`.
- **An unhealthy replica blocked its own rollback** (`816e0f1`). The replica controller gave the
  rollout a `running` that ignored health, so the rollback's budget kept the unhealthy `v3`
  replica as an unready one to retire last and stalled. `running_healthy` fixes it, with a test in
  `src/rollout_controller_test.zig`. `sim-c3` (seeds 1..40, 150 operations, also with
  `bad_release`), `sim-c3-teeth` and `sim-c1` pass with the first fix; `sim-c1` runs again in
  `zig build check` with both.
- After the agent restart in 4b, `all_ready` holds at once (the replicas never stopped), so that
  wait proves little beyond the identity checks before it.
